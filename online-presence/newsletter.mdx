Of everything you can build as an author, a newsletter list is the most valuable long-term asset. Social media followers are borrowed — they exist at the pleasure of a platform you don't own, subject to algorithm changes, policy shifts, and the possibility that the platform simply ceases to exist. A newsletter list is yours. The people on it have actively asked to hear from you, and you can reach them directly, in their inbox, for as long as they choose to stay subscribed. Building that list slowly and steadily, starting now, is one of the most worthwhile things you can do for your writing career.

## Choosing a Newsletter Platform

Several excellent platforms cater to authors and independent writers. The right choice depends on how you plan to use it and whether you want your newsletter and website in the same place.

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="Ghost">
    **Ghost** combines your author website and newsletter into a single platform, with no separate integrations required. It's the same platform that powers hawberkpress.com. Ghost is designed for writers — the editing experience is clean and focused, the reading experience is excellent, and it supports both free and paid subscriptions if you ever want to offer exclusive content.

    | | |
    |---|---|
    | **Cost** | Free tier available (Ghost.org managed hosting starts at $9/month; self-hosting is free but requires setup) |
    | **Ease of use** | Straightforward once set up; excellent writing editor |
    | **Key features** | Website + newsletter in one; free and paid tiers; member management; no per-subscriber fees on self-hosted |
    | **Best for** | Authors who want everything in one place and a clean, literary aesthetic |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Substack">
    **Substack** has become the default home for independent writers and has a built-in discovery network that can help new readers find you. It's free to use for free newsletters (Substack takes a 10% cut if you add paid subscriptions). Setup is minimal — you can have a newsletter live in under an hour.

    | | |
    |---|---|
    | **Cost** | Free for free newsletters; 10% fee on paid subscription revenue |
    | **Ease of use** | Extremely simple — minimal setup required |
    | **Key features** | Built-in discovery and recommendations; podcast support; Notes (short-form feed); no separate website needed |
    | **Best for** | Authors who want to start quickly and benefit from Substack's reader network |
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Mailchimp / Kit">
    **Mailchimp** is the long-established choice with a generous free tier and extensive automation features. **Kit** (formerly ConvertKit) is purpose-built for creators and authors, with powerful tagging and segmentation tools that let you send targeted emails to different parts of your audience.

    | | |
    |---|---|
    | **Cost** | Mailchimp: free up to 500 subscribers, then paid tiers. Kit: free up to 10,000 subscribers, paid for advanced features |
    | **Ease of use** | Moderate — more settings and options than Substack or Ghost |
    | **Key features** | Advanced automation; audience segmentation; landing pages; integrates with most website platforms |
    | **Best for** | Authors who want fine-grained control over their list and don't need a built-in discovery network |
  </Tab>

## What to Write

The most common reason authors don't send newsletters is not knowing what to say. In practice, there's rarely a shortage of material once you start looking for it.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="New Publications" icon="book-open">
    Announce new releases, upcoming titles, and pre-orders. Include the cover, a brief description, and a direct link to purchase. This is the most important thing your list is for.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Work in Progress" icon="pen-nib">
    Brief, non-spoilery glimpses of what you're working on. Readers who've subscribed to hear from you are genuinely interested in your process — don't underestimate that.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Influences and Inspirations" icon="star">
    The books, films, places, images, and ideas that feed your writing. For readers of dark speculative fiction, this is often as interesting as the work itself.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Curated Recommendations" icon="list">
    Books and stories you've loved recently. Recommending other authors generously builds goodwill and often leads to reciprocal mentions.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Tip>
  Don't wait until you have something to announce before you send your first issue. Write a short introductory letter — who you are, what you write, what subscribers can expect — and send it to whoever is already on your list. Getting into the habit of writing the newsletter matters more than having news to share.
</Tip>

## How Often to Send

Monthly is a sensible default for most authors. It's frequent enough to keep readers warm, infrequent enough that you're not scrambling for content every week.

What matters far more than frequency is **consistency**. A newsletter that arrives reliably on the first Tuesday of every month trains readers to expect it. One that arrives at unpredictable intervals — sometimes twice in a week, then not for three months — loses subscribers' trust regardless of how good the content is.

Choose a cadence you can sustain even during a difficult writing month, and stick to it. If something genuinely prevents you from sending, a brief note explaining the gap is always better than silence.

<Note>
  If you're between publications and find monthly too demanding, a quarterly newsletter is entirely respectable. Some of the most engaging author newsletters go out four times a year and are all the more anticipated for it.
</Note>

## Growing Your List

Your list grows fastest through a combination of visibility and incentive. Make it easy to find and give people a reason to sign up today rather than someday.

**Where to add your signup:**
- Prominently on your author website homepage
- In the footer of your website on every page
- In your social media bios, with a direct link to the signup page
- In the back matter of your published books (a short note pointing readers to your newsletter)
- In any guest posts or essays you write for other publications

**Offering a signup incentive:**

A free exclusive short story, a deleted scene, or the first chapter of an unpublished work is the most effective incentive for dark fiction readers. It costs you nothing beyond what you've already written, and it immediately demonstrates the quality of your work to a new subscriber.

## Subject Lines and Open Rates

The subject line is the only thing standing between your carefully written newsletter and an unopened email. A few principles worth keeping in mind:

- **Be specific rather than vague.** "A new story is out" is weaker than "My new novella is live — plus what I've been reading in August."
- **Use your name or publication title consistently** so subscribers recognise it in a crowded inbox.
- **Curiosity works, but don't mislead.** Readers who feel baited by a subject line will unsubscribe.
- **Short subject lines (under 50 characters)** display fully on most mobile clients and tend to perform well.

An open rate of 30–40% is healthy for an author newsletter. Don't chase vanity metrics — a small, engaged list is more valuable than a large, indifferent one.

## Legal Basics: GDPR and CAN-SPAM

Sending a newsletter comes with a small number of non-negotiable legal requirements. These aren't onerous, and most reputable platforms handle the technical side automatically.

<Warning>
  Always include a working **unsubscribe link** in every email you send. Most platforms add this automatically, but check that it functions. Sending to people who have not consented to receive email from you, or making it difficult to unsubscribe, breaches both GDPR (if you have EU-based subscribers) and CAN-SPAM (US law). Non-compliance can result in your sending domain being blacklisted.
</Warning>

The essentials:

- **Only email people who have actively signed up.** Don't add contacts from your personal address book without their consent.
- **Identify yourself clearly.** Your name and a legitimate postal address (or the address of your newsletter provider) must appear in the footer of every email.
- **Include an unsubscribe link.** Every platform generates this automatically — don't remove it.
- **Process unsubscribes promptly.** Anyone who clicks unsubscribe must not receive further emails from you.
- **Say how you found them.** A short reminder in each email of why they're receiving it ("You signed up at yourname.com") reduces spam complaints.

If you're in any doubt about your specific situation, the ICO (UK) and the FTC (US) publish clear plain-language guidance on their websites.
