Your author website is the one corner of the internet that belongs entirely to you. Social platforms come and go, algorithms shift without warning, and newsletter providers change their terms — but your website remains yours. It's where readers come when they want to know who you are, what you've written, and what's coming next. Setting one up well takes an afternoon; maintaining it takes minutes after each new publication.

## What Your Website Needs

Before choosing a platform or buying a domain, it helps to know what you're building. A working author website doesn't need to be elaborate — it needs to be clear, current, and easy to navigate.

## Setting Up Your Website

<Steps>
  <Step title="Choose and register your domain name">
    Your domain is your address on the web. Use your author name or pen name wherever possible — `yourname.com` is the clearest option. If that's taken, try `yournameauthor.com` or `yournamewriter.com`. Keep it short, easy to spell, and easy to say aloud.

    Register your domain through a registrar such as Namecheap, Porkbun, or Google Domains. Expect to pay around £10–15 per year. Register it independently of your hosting so you can move providers without losing your address.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Choose your platform and set up hosting">
    Pick the platform that suits your needs from the options above. Most managed platforms (Ghost Pro, Squarespace, Carrd) handle hosting for you — you simply connect your domain during setup. If you choose self-hosted WordPress or Ghost, you'll need a hosting provider such as SiteGround or DigitalOcean.

    Follow your platform's own setup guide to get the site running. Most have step-by-step onboarding that takes less than an hour.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your bio and about page">
    Write two versions of your biography. The first is a third-person bio of 100–150 words for press and promotional use — straightforward, accurate, and mentioning your published titles. The second is a first-person version for the about page, warmer in tone and more personal.

    Add a professional author photo. It doesn't need to be a studio portrait — a well-lit, clear photograph taken on a modern phone is perfectly suitable.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Build your bibliography page">
    Create a dedicated page listing all your published works. For each title, include the cover image, a short description, publication date, and a link to where readers can buy or download it. Link to the Hawberk Press catalogue page for your titles published with us.

    This page will be updated after every new publication — keep it easy to edit.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add a contact form">
    Install or enable a contact form on a dedicated contact page. Most platforms include this as a standard feature. At minimum, collect a name, email address, and message. You don't need to publish your email address anywhere on the site.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Connect your newsletter signup">
    Add a newsletter signup form in at least two places: prominently on your homepage and at the bottom of your about page. Most newsletter platforms (Ghost, Substack, Mailchimp, Kit) provide an embed code or direct integration. Make the value clear — tell visitors what they'll receive and roughly how often.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Set up your homepage">
    Your homepage should answer three questions immediately: who you are, what you write, and what a visitor should do next. A short introduction, your latest book with a cover image and buy link, and a newsletter signup are the essentials. Don't bury the most important information below the fold.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Go live and share the link">
    Once the core pages are in place, publish the site. Share your URL with Hawberk Press so we can link to it from your author page, and add it to your social profiles and newsletter footer.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Keeping Your Website Current

A neglected website sends the wrong signal to readers and industry contacts. The good news is that keeping it current only requires action at predictable moments.

**After each new publication**, do the following:
- Add the new title to your bibliography page with cover, description, and buy link
- Update your author bio if the new book changes how you introduce yourself
- Write a short announcement post or news item if your platform supports it
- Update the "latest book" feature on your homepage

<Note>
  Let us know your website URL when you have it live. We'll add it to your author profile on the Hawberk Press website and include it in any press materials we prepare for your titles.
</Note>

## Connecting Your Website to Your Newsletter

Your website and newsletter are your two most durable long-term assets as an author — treat them as a pair. Every page of your website should make it easy to subscribe. Every newsletter issue should link back to your website for fuller information about your books.

If you're using Ghost for your website, the newsletter is already built in. For other platforms, embed a signup form from your newsletter provider and place it on the homepage, the about page, and at the foot of any blog posts you write. The goal is to convert curious readers into subscribers who've actively chosen to hear from you.
