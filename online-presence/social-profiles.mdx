Social media is not the most important part of your author platform — your website and newsletter are — but it is how many readers will first encounter you, and a well-maintained profile does quiet work on your behalf every day. The key is to choose your platforms deliberately rather than trying to be everywhere at once, set each profile up properly from the start, and then show up consistently rather than brilliantly. For authors of dark speculative fiction, a small number of platforms are genuinely worth your time.

## Platforms Worth Your Time

Not every social platform is equally useful for literary fiction authors, and spreading yourself across all of them is a reliable route to burnout. The four below have the most active communities for dark, strange, and speculative fiction.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="X / Twitter" icon="x-twitter">
    **Still the core literary conversation.** Despite the turbulence of recent years, a significant community of authors, editors, reviewers, and readers of speculative fiction remains on X. The discoverability of new writers is lower than it once was, but the conversations are still happening. Worth maintaining even if it's not your primary platform.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Bluesky" icon="cloud">
    **The most active literary migration destination.** A large portion of the book and speculative fiction community has moved to Bluesky since 2023, and it has the atmosphere of early Twitter — relatively chronological, less algorithmic, and more conversational. Strongly recommended as a primary or co-primary platform for genre fiction authors.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Instagram" icon="instagram">
    **Visual, cover-forward, and community-rich.** The #bookstagram community is large and enthusiastic about genre fiction. Instagram rewards consistency and aesthetics — atmospheric images, cover reveals, and reading photographs perform well. Less about conversation, more about visual presence and discoverability.
  </Card>
  <Card title="TikTok (BookTok)" icon="video">
    **The highest-reach discovery platform for fiction.** BookTok has launched careers and driven significant sales for genre fiction, particularly horror, dark fantasy, and gothic work. It requires more effort — video content rather than text or images — but the potential reach for the right kind of book is unmatched. Consider it if you're comfortable on camera or willing to experiment.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Info>
  You don't need to be on all four. Many successful authors maintain one primary platform — the one they enjoy most and use most naturally — and treat the others as secondary or dormant presences. Pick the one that suits your voice and commit to it.
</Info>

## Profile Essentials

Wherever you have a presence, each profile should include the same core elements. A reader who finds you on any platform should immediately understand who you are and what you write.

**Author photo.** Use the same photo across all platforms. A clear, recognisable image — not a logo, not a book cover — builds recognition over time. It doesn't need to be a formal portrait, but it should be consistent.

**Bio that mentions your genre and your current book.** You have limited characters to work with on most platforms. Use them efficiently: your name, what you write (dark speculative fiction / horror / gothic fiction — whatever is most accurate), and your latest book with a link or title. Update this after every new publication.

**Link to your website or latest publication.** Most platforms allow one link in your bio. Point it to your author website as a default, or to a link-in-bio page (such as Linktree or a custom page on your website) that lists your book, newsletter signup, and other profiles.

<Tip>
  Create a simple link-in-bio page on your website rather than using a third-party service. A page at `yourname.com/links` that you control is more durable and keeps readers in your own ecosystem. Most website platforms make this straightforward to set up.
</Tip>

## Consistent Username Strategy

Wherever possible, use the same handle across all platforms. A consistent username — ideally your author name or pen name — makes you easier to find, easier to tag, and easier to recognise.

Before choosing a handle, search for your preferred name on all the platforms you intend to use. If `@yourname` is taken on one platform, consider a consistent alternative such as `@yournameauthor` or `@yournamewriter` rather than using different handles in different places.

<Note>
  Even if you don't plan to use a platform actively right now, it's worth creating an account to reserve your preferred username. Claiming `@yourname` on Bluesky, Instagram, TikTok, and X costs nothing and protects your author identity.
</Note>

## Setting Up Your Profiles

<Steps>
  <Step title="Secure your username on all platforms">
    Before anything else, create accounts on X, Bluesky, Instagram, and TikTok using your preferred author handle. Even if you don't plan to post actively on all four, reserve the name. Note down the login credentials somewhere secure.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Upload your author photo">
    Use the same photo on every platform. Crop it appropriately for each platform's profile image shape (most use a circle). A consistent face across platforms is how readers recognise you when they encounter you in a new place.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write your bio">
    Draft a short bio that fits the most constrained platform first (X allows 160 characters for its bio). Include: your name if it's different from your handle, what you write, and your latest book. Once you have a version that works in 160 characters, adapt it with a little more detail for platforms that give you more space (Instagram allows 150 characters; Bluesky allows 256).
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your link">
    On every platform, add your website URL or link-in-bio page to the dedicated link field. If a platform doesn't have a dedicated link field, include it in the bio text itself. This is the single most important function of a social profile — directing interested readers somewhere you own.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Pin your most important post">
    Once you've made a few posts, pin the most important one to the top of your profile. This is typically either an announcement post for your latest book or a post inviting readers to sign up for your newsletter. The pinned post is what new visitors see first — make it count.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Link your profiles from your website">
    Add links to all your active social profiles on your author website's about page, contact page, and footer. Readers should be able to find all of your presences from a single place.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Choosing Your Main Platform

Having a primary platform — the one you post on most often, engage with most actively, and invest the most energy in — is a more sustainable approach than trying to maintain an identical presence everywhere.

Choose your main platform based on:
- **Where you naturally enjoy spending time.** Sustainability depends on this.
- **Where your target readers are.** For dark speculative fiction, Bluesky and Instagram are both strong options right now.
- **What kind of content you create most easily.** Text comes naturally to most authors; video is harder but reaches further.

Maintain your secondary profiles with occasional posts and keep the bio and link current, but don't feel obliged to match your main platform's activity everywhere.

## Keeping Your Profiles Updated

Social profiles have a habit of going stale. Set a reminder to review each of your profiles after every new publication:

- Update your bio to reference your latest book
- Change the link if you've created a new landing page for the new title
- Write a pinned post announcing the new publication and replace the previous pin
- Upload the new cover image to any visual platforms

<Warning>
  An outdated bio that references your previous book as your "debut" or "latest" — especially if that book came out several years ago — signals to readers and industry contacts that your profile is abandoned. A current bio takes five minutes to update and makes a meaningful difference to how you're perceived.
</Warning>
