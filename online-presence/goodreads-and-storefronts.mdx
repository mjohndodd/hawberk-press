When readers finish one of your books and want to find more of your work, they typically go to one of two places: Goodreads to see what else you've written and what other readers think, or the storefront where they bought the book to browse your back catalogue. Claiming your author pages on these platforms costs nothing and takes an hour or two across all of them — but it ensures that what readers find is accurate, complete, and represents you well. Leaving these pages unclaimed means you have no control over what appears there.

## Goodreads

Goodreads is where readers track their reading, write reviews, and discover new authors. Your Goodreads author page is often the first result when someone searches your name, and it aggregates reviews of all your work in one place. Claiming it gives you the ability to manage your bio, photo, and bibliography directly.

### Setting Up the Goodreads Author Programme

<Steps>
  <Step title="Create a Goodreads account (or sign in)">
    Go to [goodreads.com](https://www.goodreads.com) and create a reader account if you don't already have one. Use your author name as your display name. If you have an existing reader account, you can use that — the author programme layers on top of your reader profile.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Find your book on Goodreads">
    Search for one of your published titles on Goodreads. Your Hawberk Press titles should already be listed — publishers and readers often add books to Goodreads before authors claim their profiles. If your book isn't listed yet, you can add it manually through the Goodreads Librarians group, or request it through the site's book-addition form.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Locate your author profile page">
    On your book's Goodreads page, click your author name to reach your author profile. At the bottom of that page, look for a link that reads **"Is this you? Let us know!"** Click it to begin the claiming process.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Apply to join the Author Programme">
    You'll be prompted to confirm that the profile belongs to you and to complete a short application. Goodreads will ask for your name, a link to a website or social profile that confirms your identity as the author, and a brief note. The review process typically takes a few days.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Receive confirmation and access your dashboard">
    Once approved, you'll receive an email and your account will be upgraded to an Author account. You'll now see an **Author Dashboard** in your navigation, giving you access to edit your bio, upload a photo, and manage your bibliography.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete your author profile">
    From your Author Dashboard, fill in the following:

    - **Author bio**: Write a clear, informative biography — similar to the third-person bio on your website. Mention your genre, your published titles, and any relevant background.
    - **Author photo**: Upload the same professional photo you use on your website and social profiles.
    - **Website**: Add the URL of your author website.
    - **Social profiles**: Add links to your Twitter/X, Instagram, Bluesky, and any other active profiles.
    - **Genre**: Select the genres most relevant to your work so Goodreads can surface you in the right recommendations.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your books to your bibliography">
    From your dashboard, ensure all your published works are listed under your name and marked as authored by you. If any books are missing or attributed incorrectly, use the Goodreads Librarians group or the site's edit function to correct them. Each book should have the correct cover, description, publication date, and edition information.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Tip>
  Once you have an Author account, you can add a **Goodreads Author Widget** to your website — a small badge showing your latest book and average rating. Find this in your Author Dashboard under widgets. It's a subtle social proof signal for visitors to your website.
</Tip>

## Amazon Author Central

Amazon Author Central gives you control over your author page on Amazon — the page that aggregates all your titles and appears in Amazon search results for your name. Claiming it is straightforward and allows you to add a biography, author photo, and additional content.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Go to Amazon Author Central">
    Visit [authorcentral.amazon.com](https://authorcentral.amazon.com) (or the equivalent for your region: .co.uk, .de, etc.). Sign in with your Amazon account, or create one if you don't have one. Note that Author Central accounts are region-specific — if your books are sold in multiple Amazon regions, you may want to claim your page in each.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Add your books">
    On the Author Central dashboard, use the search tool to find your published titles and add them to your profile. Search by title and confirm each one. If a book doesn't appear, it may not yet be live on Amazon — wait until it's available for sale, or contact Author Central support.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Complete your author page">
    Add the following to your Author Central profile:

    - **Biography**: A clear author bio in the third person. Amazon gives you substantial space — use it well, but don't pad it unnecessarily.
    - **Author photo**: Upload your standard author photo.
    - **Social media links**: Add links to your website, blog, or social profiles where prompted.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  Amazon Author Central pages are region-specific. A profile claimed on amazon.com does not automatically appear on amazon.co.uk. If a significant portion of your readership is in the UK, consider claiming both. The process is identical on each regional site.
</Note>

## Apple Books for Authors

Apple Books has its own author profile system for authors published through Apple Books Connect. Because Hawberk Press distributes through multiple channels, your titles should appear on Apple Books — claiming your profile there gives you a presence on one of the major retail platforms.

Visit [authors.apple.com](https://authors.apple.com) to claim or create your Apple Books author profile. You'll need an Apple ID. The process involves confirming your identity as an author by linking to your published works, then completing a bio and photo. Apple's author profiles appear on your book pages in the Books app and on the web.

<Tip>
  Apple Books author profiles are relatively underutilised by authors — which means that a well-completed profile stands out more than it would on a more crowded platform. Take the time to fill in the full bio and add a high-resolution author photo.
</Tip>

## Kobo Writing Life

If Hawberk Press distributes your titles through Kobo — and for digital-first publishing, Kobo is an important channel — you can add author information to your Kobo profile through **Kobo Writing Life**. Visit [kobowritinglife.com](https://www.kobowritinglife.com) and create an account or sign in. From the dashboard, you can update your author bio and photo, which will appear on your books' Kobo pages.

## Keeping Everything Consistent

The most important thing across all of these platforms is **consistency of name and image**. Use exactly the same author name on every storefront — the same capitalisation, the same pen name, the same name as it appears on your book covers. A reader searching for you should find the same recognisable person on every platform.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="After Each New Publication" icon="rotate">
    Update your Goodreads bibliography to add the new title, confirm it appears correctly on Amazon Author Central, and check that Apple Books and Kobo have the correct edition linked to your profile.
  </Card>
  <Card title="Annual Profile Review" icon="calendar">
    Once a year, review all your storefront profiles to ensure your bio is current, your photo is still the one you're using elsewhere, and all your titles are correctly listed with accurate information.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

<Warning>
  If your author name on any storefront differs from the name on your book covers — even by a small inconsistency like a missing middle initial or inconsistent capitalisation — it can cause your books to appear under a different author entry, splitting your bibliography across multiple profiles. Check each platform carefully and use the platform's correction or contact process to fix any discrepancies promptly.
</Warning>
