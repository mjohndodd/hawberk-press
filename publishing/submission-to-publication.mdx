Congratulations — your story has been accepted, and the work of bringing it into the world begins now. The path from accepted manuscript to published work involves several distinct stages, each one building on the last. Understanding this pipeline helps you know what to expect, when to expect it, and how best to contribute at each step. For short fiction, the typical journey from signed contract to publication runs **two to four months**, depending on editorial complexity, production schedules, and platform lead times.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Submission Accepted">
    You'll receive an offer letter from the editorial team confirming that your story has been selected for publication. This letter will outline the key terms of the agreement — rights, payment structure, and any relevant publication details. Read it carefully, and don't hesitate to ask questions before moving forward. Once you're ready to proceed, you'll be sent a formal publishing contract for your review and signature.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Contract Signed">
    When both parties have countersigned the agreement, your story officially enters the Hawberk Press publication queue. The signed contract is your record of the terms — keep a copy somewhere safe. Editorial work begins as soon as the agreement is in place, so this stage marks the true start of the collaborative process.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Editorial Rounds">
    Your story will move through one or more rounds of editorial feedback. Depending on the work, this may include developmental notes on structure and character, followed by line edits for prose clarity and rhythm, and finally a close copyedit for consistency and correctness. Each round is a conversation between you and the editorial team — you'll have the opportunity to review all suggested changes and respond to notes before anything is finalised. See the [Editorial Process](/publishing/editorial-process) guide for a detailed look at how this stage works.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Production">
    Once the manuscript is editorially approved, your story moves into production. This is where the visual and technical work happens: cover art is commissioned, metadata (title, author name, description, categories, keywords) is prepared, and the final text is formatted and packaged for eBook distribution. You may be consulted on certain metadata decisions — particularly the story description and author bio — so keep an eye on your inbox during this period.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Pre-Publication Review">
    Before anything goes live, you'll receive a final proof of the formatted eBook and an opportunity to review the cover art and metadata. This is your last chance to catch any errors or raise concerns. Please turn around your approval or feedback promptly, as production schedules depend on this step completing in a timely way.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Publication">
    Your story goes live across Hawberk Press's digital distribution platforms. You'll receive a notification from the editorial team with direct links to your published work so you can share it with readers and add it to your bibliography. Publication day is a milestone worth marking — we're proud to have your work in the catalogue.
  </Step>

  <Step title="Post-Publication">
    Publication isn't the end of the relationship — it's the beginning of the next phase. The editorial team will support ongoing promotion of your story, and your royalty reporting cycle begins from the publication date. You'll receive regular statements detailing your earnings. See the [Royalties & Payments](/publishing/royalties-and-payments) guide for full details on how and when you're paid.
  </Step>
</Steps>

<Note>
  The two-to-four month estimate reflects typical short fiction timelines under normal conditions. Anthology projects, longer works, or stories requiring significant editorial attention may take longer. Your editor will keep you informed of the schedule specific to your publication and will flag any changes as early as possible.
</Note>
