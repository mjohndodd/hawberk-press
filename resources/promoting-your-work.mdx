Publishing with Hawberk Press means your story reaches readers through a publisher that genuinely cares about its reception — but the most powerful advocate for your work will always be you. Reader communities in the dark and speculative fiction world are passionate and word-of-mouth travels fast; a thoughtful, consistent promotion effort from you, working alongside what Hawberk Press does centrally, gives your story the best possible start.

## The Promotion Toolkit

Hawberk Press and you each bring different things to your book's promotion. Understanding the division clearly helps you focus your energy where it matters most.

## Key Promotion Windows

Not all moments are equal. Dark speculative fiction has natural rhythms in the publishing calendar, and your promotion efforts will go further if you concentrate them at the right times.

### Launch Week

The first seven days after publication are the most important. Retail algorithms reward early sales velocity, and reader attention is at its peak when your story is new. This is the week to post, share, and engage most actively. Coordinate timing with the Hawberk Press editorial team so your personal posts reinforce — not duplicate — the press's own announcements.

### The One-Month Milestone

After the initial excitement fades, many authors go quiet. Don't. The one-month mark is a natural moment to reflect publicly on your story — share a favourite line, write something about the themes that drove you, or simply let people know the book is still out there and you're proud of it. This second wave of activity often reaches readers who missed the launch.

### Seasonal Opportunities

Dark fiction has a calendar. Halloween is the obvious peak — the weeks of October are when readers actively seek out the strange and unsettling. But consider other windows: the midwinter reading season, the long dark stretch of January, and any cultural moment that resonates with your story's themes. A well-timed post connecting your work to a relevant season or news moment can bring new eyes to a story published months ago.

## Coordinating with Hawberk Press

Before you launch your own promotion push, speak with the editorial team. They can share:

- The planned schedule for Hawberk Press's own launch posts
- Which major review outlets they're approaching centrally (to avoid duplicate pitching)
- Any approved promotional copy, cover images, or excerpt selections
- Retail links in the formats best suited for sharing

<Note>
  Avoid announcing your publication date or sharing cover images publicly before Hawberk Press has given you the go-ahead. Coordinated launches land better than staggered ones.
</Note>

## Low-Effort, High-Impact Activities

You don't need to become a full-time marketer. A handful of well-chosen actions, done consistently, will outperform a frantic burst of posts that quickly burns out.

<Steps>
  <Step title="Share the link — simply and personally">
    Post the retail link to your story with a sentence or two in your own voice. Not marketing copy. Just you, telling people you wrote something and it's out now. Readers respond to honesty over polish.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Write one short piece about the story">
    A paragraph or a post about what drew you to this subject, what unsettled you about it, or what you hope readers feel. This gives people a reason to be curious before they've read a word of the fiction itself.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Engage with your earliest readers">
    When someone finishes your story and says something about it — publicly or privately — respond. Thank them. Ask what they thought. These early readers become word-of-mouth advocates if you treat the conversation as real.
  </Step>
  <Step title="Tag Hawberk Press in relevant posts">
    When you share on social media, tag @hawberkpress so the press can amplify your post to its own audience. A combined signal reaches further than either of you alone.
  </Step>
</Steps>

## Medium-Effort Activities

Once you've covered the basics, these activities extend your reach into communities and audiences you wouldn't otherwise touch.

### Pitching Review Blogs and Genre Sites

The dark fiction and weird fiction communities have a healthy ecosystem of independent reviewers and bloggers. A genuine, personalised pitch — not a mass email — can land your story in front of a dedicated readership. See the [Press & Reviews guide](/resources/press-and-reviews) for how to approach this well.

### Newsletter Mentions

If you have a mailing list of any size, tell your subscribers. Even a small, engaged list of people who already like your writing is a high-value audience. If you don't have a list yet, this is a good moment to consider starting one — see [Reader Engagement](/resources/reader-engagement) for options.

### Podcast Appearances

Genre fiction podcasts — particularly those covering horror, weird fiction, and dark speculative work — regularly feature authors for interviews and readings. A short excerpt read aloud on a fiction podcast can introduce your work to hundreds of listeners who have never heard of Hawberk Press. Start with shows you already listen to and admire; cold pitches to relevant programmes are generally well received when the story fits the show.

<Tip>
  Keep a simple log of every outreach you make — reviewer name, platform, date contacted, outcome. It takes five minutes to set up and saves considerable confusion once you have several irons in the fire.
</Tip>
