Your author bio and headshot are small things that carry disproportionate weight. They appear on your book's back matter, your author page, every review pitch you send, and every social profile a new reader might find. Consistency across all of those places — the same name, the same voice, the same photograph — builds recognisability and trust over time. This guide walks you through what you need, how to write it well, and how to keep it working for you.

## Why Consistency Matters

When a reader encounters your name for the first time and then goes looking for you, they are building a mental picture. If your bio on your website describes you differently from your bio on your retailer page, and your headshot is three different photographs from three different decades, the picture they form is blurry. A consistent author identity — same name, same essential story, same face — makes you easier to find, easier to remember, and easier to trust.

<Info>
  If you write under a pen name, choose it carefully and commit to it. Use your pen name consistently across every public-facing platform — your author page, social media, retailer profiles, and all press materials. Your Hawberk Press author page will list whatever name you specify; just make sure you tell your editorial contact which name to use.
</Info>

## The Two Bio Lengths You Need

Every author should maintain two versions of their bio: a short version for quick-reference contexts, and a longer version for press and professional submissions.

### Short Bio — approximately 50 words

Your short bio is for social media profiles, podcast guest introductions, author newsletter signatures, and anywhere space is at a premium. It should cover the essentials and nothing more: who you are, what you write, and one memorable detail.

### Long Bio — 150 to 200 words

Your long bio is for press kits, your author website, Hawberk Press's author page, and anywhere you have room to give a fuller picture. It follows the same structure as the short bio but expands each element — more texture on your writing, more context on your publication history, a slightly fuller personal note.

## What to Include in Your Bio

A strong author bio, at either length, covers four things:

1. **Your name** — the name you publish under, stated clearly at or near the start
2. **Your genre and style** — what you write and, briefly, how you write it ("atmospheric dark fiction," "weird horror in the Chambers tradition," "unsettling speculative short stories")
3. **Notable publications** — your Hawberk Press title or titles, and any prior publications that are genuinely relevant (a story in a well-regarded magazine, a previous book, a notable prize)
4. **One personal detail** — something real and specific that gives the bio a human quality without becoming an overshare. Where you live, something you're obsessed with, an unusual fact.

## What to Avoid

<Warning>
  **Don't call yourself an aspiring writer.** You are a published author. Hawberk Press has contracted and published your work. Own it.
</Warning>

- **Vague, generic filler** — phrases like "has always loved stories" or "believes in the power of imagination" say nothing and dilute the bio
- **A CV listing** — a list of every workshop attended, every rejection overcome, and every minor publication credit reads as insecure and buries what matters
- **Third-person inconsistency** — choose either first or third person and use it throughout. Third person ("Jane Smith writes dark fiction…") is conventional for press use and recommended for your Hawberk Press materials. First person ("I write dark fiction…") can work well on a personal website.
- **An outdated bio** — if your bio still doesn't mention your Hawberk Press book after it's published, update it immediately

## Bio Templates

Use these templates as starting points. Fill in the bracketed sections with your own details and adjust the phrasing so it sounds like you.

**Short bio template (approximately 50 words):**

```
[Author Name] writes [genre/style description] fiction. [His/Her/Their] work has appeared in [publication name(s)] and [his/her/their] debut/latest [novel/collection/novella], [Title], is published by Hawberk Press. [He/She/They] lives in [location] and [one brief personal detail — e.g., "has a weakness for early twentieth-century ghost stories and very strong tea"].
```

**Long bio template (150–200 words):**

```
[Author Name] is a writer of [genre/style] fiction with a particular interest in [specific thematic territory — e.g., "the uncanny edges of the everyday" or "the Gothic imagination of the early twentieth century"].

[His/Her/Their] [novel/novella/collection], [Title], is published by Hawberk Press and described as [one-sentence description of the book]. Prior work has appeared in [list of publications, or "various genre publications"], and [he/she/they] [has/have] been [any relevant recognition — shortlisted for X, reviewed in Y — or omit this sentence if not applicable].

[Author Name] is drawn to fiction that [says something about the kind of stories you're drawn to and why — make this specific and honest rather than generic]. [He/She/They] believes the best dark fiction [a genuine sentence about your perspective on the genre — optional but valuable if you can make it interesting].

[He/She/They] lives in [location] [with/and] [brief personal detail — family, an obsession, a habit, something real]. You can find [him/her/them] at [website or primary social profile].
```

## Headshot Guidelines

Your headshot doesn't need to be taken by a professional photographer, but it does need to do a specific job: show your face clearly, present you as a writer worth taking seriously, and be current enough to be recognisable in person.

<CardGroup cols={2}>
  <Card title="What works well" icon="circle-check">
    A clear, well-lit image of your face from roughly shoulder height up. Natural light or soft indoor light both work. The background should be uncluttered. You can lean into atmosphere — a slightly moody or literary quality fits Hawberk Press's aesthetic — as long as your face is clearly visible.
  </Card>
  <Card title="What to avoid" icon="circle-xmark">
    Group photos where you've cropped everyone else out. Holiday snapshots. Images more than two or three years out of date. Heavily filtered images that don't look like you. Very dark images where your face is hard to read. Pet photos standing in for a headshot.
  </Card>
</CardGroup>

**Technical requirements for your Hawberk Press author page:**
- Format: JPEG or PNG
- Minimum dimensions: 800 × 800 px
- Orientation: square or portrait (landscape crops poorly in author page layouts)
- File size: under 5 MB

## Submitting Your Bio and Headshot to Hawberk Press

To have your bio and headshot published on your Hawberk Press author page, email both to your editorial contact with the subject line **"Author page materials — [Your Name]"**.

Include:
- Your bio in the body of the email (so it can be copied directly without reformatting)
- Your headshot as an attached file at the correct dimensions
- Confirmation of the name you'd like to appear on the page (pen name or legal name)
- A link to your author website or primary social profile, if you'd like it included

<Tip>
  Update your bio whenever something significant changes — a new publication, a prize, a significant shift in what you're writing. A bio that accurately reflects your current work is always more useful than one that's technically accurate but five years stale.
</Tip>
