Social media works best for authors when it feels like a genuine extension of your creative personality rather than a marketing chore. The readers drawn to dark speculative fiction are a discerning lot — they respond to authenticity, curiosity, and a sense that there's a real human being behind the work. What follows is practical guidance for the platforms where that community lives, and for the habits that sustain a presence over time without exhausting you.

## Which Platforms Matter

You don't need to be everywhere. The dark fiction and weird fiction communities have meaningful presences on a handful of platforms, and spreading yourself thinly across all of them is far less effective than being genuinely present on one or two.

## What to Post

<Tabs>
  <Tab title="X / Twitter">
    **Cover reveals and announcements**
    Share your cover image when Hawberk Press gives you the go-ahead. Pair it with a single atmospheric sentence from the story — not a plot summary.

    **Short excerpt posts**
    A striking opening line, an unsettling image, a sentence that captures the story's mood. Keep it short enough to read in the timeline without clicking through.

    **Process and reflection**
    What drew you to this subject? What research surprised you? What were you afraid of while writing it? These posts travel further than buy-links.

    **Engagement with the community**
    Reply to other authors and readers discussing dark fiction. Quote-post reviews with a gracious acknowledgement. Join the conversations you find interesting — not only the ones that mention you.

    **Hashtags to use**
    `#darkfiction` `#specfic` `#WeirdFiction` `#horror` `#shortstory` — and `#KingInYellow` where your story's themes genuinely connect to that tradition.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Instagram">
    **Cover imagery**
    Instagram is a visual medium. A clean image of your cover, well-lit or with a simple atmospheric background, performs well. Use the caption for something personal — not marketing language.

    **Atmospheric photography**
    If you have a visual sensibility, photograph things that feel like your story: fog, old architecture, candlelight, strange found objects. Caption them with a line from the work.

    **Story carousels**
    A multi-image post with an excerpt broken across slides gives readers a reason to engage and swipe through. Use your cover as the first image.

    **Behind-the-scenes glimpses**
    Your writing space, your research materials, the books on your shelf that fed into this story. Readers are genuinely curious about the texture of a writer's life.

    **Hashtags to use**
    `#darkfiction` `#WeirdFiction` `#horrorfiction` `#speculativefiction` `#indiepress` `#bookstagram` — keep it to five or six; more looks cluttered.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Bluesky">
    **Conversation over broadcast**
    Bluesky's literary community is especially receptive to genuine discussion. Lead with a thought or a question rather than a link. The link can come second.

    **Thread-style reflection**
    Bluesky supports short threads well. A three- or four-post thread about the weird fiction tradition, or about what drew you to the King in Yellow aesthetic, will find an audience that cares.

    **Cross-posting with care**
    Don't simply mirror everything you post on X. Bluesky has a distinct culture and readers will notice if posts feel like automated duplicates. Adapt the tone slightly.

    **Community building**
    Follow and engage with the #WeirdFiction and #DarkFiction feeds. The community is smaller and more intimate than X, which makes genuine connection easier.
  </Tab>
  <Tab title="Reddit">
    **The right subreddits**
    Focus your attention on communities where your work genuinely belongs:
    - **r/WeirdFiction** — for fiction in the Chambers / Ligotti / VanderMeer tradition
    - **r/horrorlit** — readers looking for recommendations and discussion
    - **r/shortstory** — if your Hawberk Press publication is short fiction
    - **r/DarkFantasy** — for work with darker fantasy elements

    **Be a community member first**
    Reddit readers are particularly sensitive to authors appearing only to promote themselves. Spend time commenting on other posts, joining discussions, and being genuinely useful before you post about your own work.

    **How to share your work**
    Most genre subreddits permit self-promotion posts, but read each community's rules carefully. A post that says "I wrote a weird fiction story about [theme] and it's available here, happy to answer questions about the writing process" performs far better than a bare buy-link.

    **AMA and Q&A**
    Once you have a few readers who can vouch for the work, a short informal AMA (Ask Me Anything) in a relevant subreddit can be a genuinely enjoyable way to connect with readers.
  </Tab>
</Tabs>

## Frequency and Consistency

The single most common mistake authors make on social media is posting intensely around their launch and then going silent. Algorithms deprioritise accounts that fall dormant, but more importantly, readers forget. A modest, consistent presence — three or four posts a week across your chosen platforms — will serve you better than daily posting at launch followed by weeks of quiet.

<Tip>
  If you find a posting schedule hard to maintain, draft several posts in a single sitting and schedule them out over a fortnight. Most platforms support native scheduling; tools like Buffer or Later work across multiple platforms if you prefer.
</Tip>

## How to Talk About Your Story Without Spoiling It

You can spend weeks discussing your work without giving anything essential away. Focus on:

- **The feeling** — what atmosphere does the story create? What emotion do you want a reader to finish it with?
- **The question** — what were you genuinely trying to work out while writing it?
- **The influences** — which authors, images, or ideas fed into it? Readers who share your influences are your most natural audience.
- **The specific and strange** — one vivid, particular detail from the story that doesn't reveal the plot but suggests the texture of the world.

## What to Avoid

<Warning>
  A few habits will actively damage your standing with readers and reviewers. Avoid them regardless of how your launch is going.
</Warning>

- **Posting only buy-links** — a feed that is nothing but "my book is available here" trains readers to ignore you.
- **Burst-posting at launch, then vanishing** — inconsistency is more damaging than low frequency.
- **Responding defensively to reviews** — even a bad review, left alone, does less damage than an author publicly contesting it.
- **Mass-tagging readers or other authors** — unsolicited tagging reads as spam and alienates the very people you want to reach.
- **Arguing about your genre or your work** — readers remember how you made them feel, not who was right in an online debate.
